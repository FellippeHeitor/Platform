[size=large][b]Leap Line[/b][/size]
[i]Tap to hop, hold to jump higher. Don't stop, don't fall.[/i]

[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/01-title.png[/img]

Leap Line is a small one-button platformer. The hero runs by itself and the only thing you control is the jump: a tap gives a short hop, holding gives a higher jump. The levels are hand-made and the same every time, so you learn each one by dying and trying again. Retrying is instant, and the game shows how far you got.

[b]Controls[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Space[/b], mouse button or touch: jump
[*][b]R[/b]: restart the attempt
[*][b]M[/b]: mute
[*][b]Esc[/b]: back to the title screen (Esc on the title quits)
[*]On the title screen, [b]Left/Right[/b] picks any level you've already played
[/list]

[b]11 levels, 5 themes[/b]
The first levels add one idea at a time: steps and gaps, spikes and low ceilings, planks you can jump up through, diamonds that give you one extra jump in mid-air (over lava, of course), switches that drop barriers, and teleporters.

[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/02-diamond-skies.png[/img]

Level 6, [b]Jungle Crossing[/b], is a nod to the jungle adventures we played on the Atari 2600. You grab vines by touching their tips and let go with a press, and you hop across crocodiles. A croc's head is always safe to stand on, but its snout only while the jaws are shut. Mind the tar pits.

[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/03-jungle-vine.png[/img]
[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/04-jungle-crocs.png[/img]

The last five levels are built around power-ups you can only use for a limited time:
[list]
[*][b]Jetpack[/b]: hold to thrust upward. Fuel is limited (a gauge shows what's left), fuel cans top it up, and when it runs out you drop.
[*][b]Wings[/b]: jump in mid-air as often as you like, for 2.5 seconds.
[/list]

[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/06-fuel-line-jetpack.png[/img]
[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/07-gauntlet-wings.png[/img]

When you die, you see the percentage you reached and what got you. A dotted line then marks where your last attempt ended, so you can see whether you're getting further.

[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/forum-post/08-death-screen.png[/img]

[b]Under the hood[/b]
[list]
[*][b]One source file runs in both QB64-PE and QBJS[/b], with no QBJS-only code. You can play it natively or in the browser.
[*][b]Fixed 60 Hz physics[/b] with an accumulator, so jumps behave the same at any frame rate. Jump height depends on how long you hold. There's a short grace period after you run off a ledge, and a press made just before landing still counts.
[*][b]All sounds and music are generated by the program[/b] from sine-wave voices. Each theme has an eight-bar tune written as note lists (like "E5:2 G5:2 C6:2"), which the game turns into a looping WAV with bass, arpeggios and drums. There's also a short victory song. A native build writes any missing WAV on first run, so only the .bas is needed.
[*][b]Every level is checked by a solver[/b]. levelcheck.bas includes the game in headless mode and searches every button timing through the game's own physics code until it finds a winning run, then replays it to confirm. It also reports how many 1/60 s ticks early or late you can press and still make it, and which pickups you actually need.
[*]Smooth anti-aliased sprites drawn in code, parallax backgrounds, particles, rippling lava and tar, and squash and stretch on the hero.
[*]Levels are DATA lines with one character per tile, so making your own is easy.
[/list]

[b]Where it started[/b]
Leap Line grew out of a little experiment I started back in February 2021, called mrJumpS. The hero was a white rectangle, the level was 30 random platforms over water, and there were no spikes, power-ups or sounds yet. You'll notice it already had the parallax mountains and the hero's shadow on the platforms. Here it is, playable, the way it was then (I only changed what QBJS needed to run it). Space or click to jump, Esc to restart:

[b]Get it / play it[/b]
Source: [url=https://github.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform]github.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform[/url] (the game is [b]mrJumpS.bas[/b])

Build natively from your QB64-PE folder:
[code]./qb64pe -x /path/to/mrJumpS.bas -o mrJumpS[/code]

Play in the browser: [url=https://qbjs.org/?src=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FellippeHeitor/Platform/master/mrJumpS.bas]run it on qbjs.org[/url]

Your progress (levels played and your best percentage on each) is saved in leapline.log next to the program.

Feedback is welcome, especially on difficulty: what was too easy, and where did you get stuck?
